# Roamly AI — UX Case Study

## Project summary

Roamly AI explores how conversational AI can reduce the effort of planning a multi-stop trip. The concept brings discovery, destination evaluation, itinerary creation, saved places, and traveller preferences into one calm mobile experience.

**Role:** UI/UX Designer  
**Focus:** Product thinking, interaction design, visual design, AI-assisted workflow  
**Platform:** Mobile-first responsive web prototype  
**Status:** Interactive concept

## The problem

Travel planning is fragmented. Travellers often move among inspiration apps, search results, maps, notes, and booking services. This creates repetitive research and makes it difficult to understand whether a trip has a realistic pace.

## The goal

Design a friendly companion that helps a traveller move from “Where should I go?” to a useful starting itinerary without removing their control.

## Target users

- Independent travellers planning leisure trips
- People who want inspiration but feel overwhelmed by research
- Travellers balancing food, culture, nature, and pace
- Mobile users who want to save ideas and return later

## Key design questions

1. How might AI feel helpful without becoming confusing or overpromising?
2. How can destination discovery lead naturally into planning?
3. How can a generated itinerary remain understandable and editable?
4. How can the interface communicate a calm, trustworthy personality?

## Design approach

### 1. A focused entry point

The Explore screen gives users two clear ways forward: browse destinations or ask Roamly AI for a plan. Mood shortcuts reduce the effort required to begin.

### 2. Natural-language planning

The planner accepts a free-form request and offers starter prompts. This supports both confident users and people who do not know how to describe their ideal trip.

### 3. Progressive detail

Destination pages surface only the most useful signals first: character, rating, reason to visit, and highlights. The primary action connects the destination to the itinerary.

### 4. A readable itinerary

The trip screen uses a day-by-day structure with a visible route and pace. The hierarchy is designed for quick scanning on a phone.

### 5. Reassuring visual language

Forest green communicates stability, lime adds optimistic energy, and soft neutral surfaces keep dense travel information approachable.

## Core user flow

```text
Explore → Ask Roamly AI → Review destination → Add to trip → View itinerary → Save places
```

## Accessibility considerations

- Semantic buttons and form labels
- Visible keyboard focus
- Live status messages for prototype feedback
- Reduced-motion support
- High-contrast primary actions
- Responsive layout from mobile to desktop

## Prototype validation

The current prototype validates navigation, information hierarchy, prompt entry, saved-place behavior, and itinerary feedback. It does not yet validate real AI output quality, booking data, authentication, or long-term trip management.

## What I would test next

- Whether users understand what AI will generate before submitting
- Whether itinerary cards provide enough information for decisions
- Whether users expect direct booking or recommendation-only behavior
- Whether saved places and saved trips need separate mental models
- How travellers edit pace, budget, and interests after generation

## Outcome

Roamly AI demonstrates a coherent end-to-end product direction and a reusable visual system. The prototype is intentionally frontend-only; future work would connect live destination data, secure AI services, authentication, and persistent trip storage.

## Next iteration

- Editable itinerary days and drag-to-reorder
- Budget and pace controls
- Map-based route preview
- Explainable AI recommendations
- Collaborative trip sharing
- Usability testing with target travellers
